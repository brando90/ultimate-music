## What writing it by hand changes

An independently recorded performance generally does not require permission to copy Metallica's original sound recording if no original recorded sounds are used. Section 114(b) distinguishes independently fixed sounds even when they imitate an existing recording. That is why rendering a score yourself materially helps. It does not dispose of rights in the composition or in any separately licensed playback sounds. [17 U.S.C. section 114(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec114.htm).

The underlying music remains a separate protected work. Entering its notes by hand can still reproduce or adapt it. A standard MIDI file contains musical instructions rather than the original recorded sounds. High-school or personal origin alone does not establish an exemption for later commercial distribution. [17 U.S.C. section 106](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec106.htm); [Copyright Office Compendium, section 803.4(C), printed page 38](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap800/ch800-performing-arts.pdf).

## Can a jazz arrangement qualify as a cover?

Yes. A change of ensemble, instrumentation or performance style is not automatically disqualifying. United States law permits arrangements needed for the style or interpretation of a cover, while limiting changes to the basic melody and fundamental character. The label "Jazz Quintet Suite" does not decide this. The finished music must be assessed; there is no reliable percentage-of-notes test. Material changes beyond that privilege can require direct composition-owner permission. [17 U.S.C. section 115(a)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec115.htm); [RouteNote's cover definition](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/what-is-a-cover-song/).

The actual candidate's saved prompt has instrumental structure tags and genre/instrument descriptions. A prompt does not prove what every passage of the output contains. References to named performers elsewhere in the blog are not proof that the exact candidate cloned a voice or used their recordings.

## The distinct Suno permission issue

The archived 03-26-2026 terms require sufficient rights in submissions for Suno's uses, including output generation and model improvement. Their Content section requires permissions covering the included copyrights and a broad sublicensable license. The paid-tier assignment concerns rights Suno owns and is conditional on compliance. Owning a rendered performance does not itself provide those rights in Metallica's composition. [Archived official Suno terms, captured 08-31-2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260831095413/https://suno.com/terms).

The archive's noncommercial Remix provision specifically concerns other users remixing a user's outputs; it should not automatically be applied to Brando transforming his own rendering. The input-rights requirement is the stronger concern. The March revision date supports, but does not conclusively establish, its applicability on the May creation date. Current September terms alone would not establish the May contract.

Official Upload Audio guidance dated 03-24-2025 also describes an ownership confirmation during upload. This independently supports taking input rights seriously. [Suno Upload Audio guidance](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2477633).

No publisher authorization, applicable Suno catalog license or other permission covering this production chain has been established in the records reviewed. That is an evidence gap, not a finding of infringement or proof the output can never be cleared. Suno support could clarify its contract and any applicable license; an ordinary support assurance is not itself permission from the composition owner.

Spotify's digital-service blanket license covers specified delivery activities and necessary service copies. It does not grant every other use. Consequently, Spotify distribution licensing is not evidence of permission for earlier, unrelated Suno processing. [17 U.S.C. section 115(d)(1)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap1-sec115.htm).

## What costs money?

RouteNote Free costs $0 upfront and has no monthly or annual distribution fee. Brando receives 85% of the net revenue RouteNote receives; RouteNote retains 15%. This revenue share is ongoing when revenue is earned, rather than a subscription bill. [RouteNote pricing](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/how-much-does-routenote-cost/).

RouteNote Premium is optional: a single costs $10 initially, followed by $9.99 per release annually. It is not the chosen plan.

RouteNote explicitly permits cover distribution to Spotify without the artist obtaining a separate mechanical license. A mechanical license concerns reproduction and distribution of the composition, distinct from rights in a particular recording. The conclusion here is limited to Spotify-only distribution; physical sales and other stores or territories can require different licensing. [RouteNote cover rights](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/how-do-i-get-the-rights-to-distribute-a-cover/).

## Why is this possible?

The original songwriters still have royalty rights. Streaming services have licensing and payment obligations for compositions. In the United States, eligible digital music services obtain blanket mechanical licenses, and the Mechanical Licensing Collective collects and distributes the corresponding royalties. Those obligations sit at the service level for covered streaming uses. [Copyright Office explanation](https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/115/); [Mechanical Licensing Collective](https://www.themlc.com/).

Separately, RouteNote funds its Free distribution service through its share of recording revenue. The 15% distributor share is not a purchase of Metallica's composition rights.

Suno says commercial rights for eligible songs made while subscribed survive cancellation; a new subscription is not required merely to retain them. A later subscription does not automatically upgrade free-period output. This does not override input-rights conditions. [Suno paid-period rights](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2425729).

A generic one-time mechanical-license purchase cannot be assumed to cover broader Suno uses or a substantially rewritten adaptation. There is no verified universal "$30 arrangement allowance". Any direct permission would need its actual scope and price checked. No purchase is recommended on the present evidence.

## Recommendation and what would resolve the gap

Keep RouteNote Free and Spotify-only as the proposed distribution route. Do not describe this specific Suno production as fully cleared solely because Brando wrote the score, had Premier and Spotify pays composition royalties.

For this exact version, establish permission covering the Suno input/use and assess the final arrangement. Useful evidence would be a relevant license from the composition owner, or confirmation identifying an applicable Suno license and its scope. If no such authority exists, direct permission may be needed. Distributor moderation approval alone does not supply it.

A new independent performance or licensed sample-library rendering without Suno avoids this particular Suno input-contract issue prospectively. It still needs an eligible arrangement and appropriate composition/distribution rights; making a new recording does not retrospectively resolve earlier uses.

RouteNote accepts some artificial-intelligence-generated music, subject to source terms and moderation. Submit truthful cover/source information if the release later proceeds, credit the original writers, and do not imply Metallica performed or endorsed it. Approval is not guaranteed. [RouteNote generated-content policy](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/can-i-upload-ai-releases/).

## DistroKid alternative: verified 11-01-2026

DistroKid is a practical alternative for releasing an eligible cover under Brando's artist name. Its cover guide expressly allows genre changes and embellishments, subject to preserving the basic melody and fundamental character. Its separate policy accepts music made with artificial intelligence, subject to distribution rights and content rules. These policies support a release path; they do not establish automatic approval of this exact Suno recording. [Cover guide](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013648953-Uploading-Cover-Songs-to-DistroKid); [generated-music policy](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/41182362733715-Can-I-Upload-Music-Made-With-AI-Tools-to-DistroKid).

The advertised United States Musician plan is $24.99 per year for one artist. Cover licensing adds $12 per cover per year, so one artist with one cover is approximately **$36.99 each year**, before taxes, optional extras or other applicable charges. This is an advertised-price calculation, not a checkout quote. It exceeds the existing one-time $30 authorization and introduces recurring charges. RouteNote Free remains the option that fits the present no-annual-fee preference. [DistroKid pricing](https://distrokid.com/product/distrokid/plans-and-pricing-2/); [cover fee](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013534274-What-Are-Album-Extras).

An eligible cover can earn recording royalties; there is no need to renounce earnings merely because it is a cover. DistroKid passes through the earnings allocated to the artist by stores, subject to applicable deductions, while its cover service handles songwriter payments. Earnings are not a guaranteed amount or a fixed small percentage of the original artist's revenue. [DistroKid earnings explanation](https://distrokid.com/product/distrokid/plans-and-pricing-2/).

**Generated-content disclosure is part of the current workflow.** DistroKid asks whether any part of a track was generated and distinguishes lyrics, composition, all audio and part of the audio. Its credits guidance includes generated instrumental performances. Answer the applicable questions accurately; using human-written source material does not make Suno-generated audio a human performance. No requirement was found in these instructions to put "Suno" in the song title or artist biography. Preserve the original human songwriter credits. [Upload questions](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/50784709021971-How-to-Fill-Out-AI-Credits); [credit definitions](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/50784235803411-What-Are-AI-Credits).

DistroKid's mechanical license covers content it distributes and is not offered for uses outside its service. It therefore does not establish permission for the earlier Suno input and processing. This is a specific scope limitation, not proof that the track cannot be released. The existing input-permission question remains documented above. [License scope](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013659693-Why-Am-I-Required-to-Purchase-My-Cover-Song-License-Through-DistroKid).

**Decision:** Both distributors offer credible routes for eligible covers. Keep RouteNote Free as the selected route under current instructions; DistroKid is a reasonable alternative if Brando later chooses its recurring pricing. No account, purchase or distributor change was made during this comparison. The final-WAV upload hold remains in effect. Private conversation excerpts were not copied into these public notes.

The existing jazz candidate is already a WAV (Waveform Audio File Format): `experiments/2026-10-27_first-release/the-day-that-never-comes-bebop-44k-16bit.wav`, 44,100 samples per second, 16-bit stereo, 181.92 seconds. Its current checksum matches the recorded candidate. If this is still Brando's intended take, no new Suno subscription is needed merely to obtain a WAV. This is a file-format finding, not a new listening approval or rights determination.

The attached `The Day That Never Comes MP3.m4a` is a different asset: its checksum exactly matches the saved Suno parent download, previously traced to the Sibelius playback, approximately 344.97 seconds. Despite its filename, it contains Opus audio in an MP4 container, not MP3 audio. Preserve it as source evidence rather than substituting it for the jazz candidate. The separate repository MP3 is approximately 188.6 kilobits per second at 48,000 samples per second. See [recording provenance](../experiments/2026-10-27_first-release/provenance-comparison-10-29-2026.json) and [jazz-file identity](../experiments/2026-10-27_first-release/incoming-audio-comparison-10-29-2026.json).

RouteNote's standard upload guidance specifies stereo FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec), or 320-kilobit-per-second MP3, at 44,100 samples per second, with 16-bit lossless audio. Its WAV guidance recommends FLAC delivery. The appropriate preparation is to encode the approved existing WAV losslessly to FLAC and verify identical decoded samples; this requires no new Suno download. Converting compressed audio to WAV does not recover detail already discarded. [RouteNote requirements](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/what-are-the-audio-file-requirements/); [WAV-to-FLAC guidance](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/what-is-wav/).

Suno lists new WAV downloads as a Pro/Premier feature. A subscription would therefore be relevant to a new download or different final take, not necessary simply because a usable WAV already exists locally. No subscription, audio conversion or upload was performed in this check. [Suno download formats](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/13926081).
