### Bio (draft)

Brando Miranda is a doctoral student at Stanford University studying artificial intelligence and a musician with a longstanding interest in jazz. At sixteen, in 2010, he arranged Metallica's "The Day That Never Comes" for his school orchestra at Greengates. Working in Sibelius, he wrote parts for strings, brass, winds and percussion, exploring how a rock song could take shape in a different ensemble.

Sixteen years later, he returned to that arrangement with Suno, an artificial intelligence music tool, to explore a bebop interpretation. His first proposed single brings that project into a small-ensemble jazz setting. The recording was generated with Suno from his arrangement; it is not a live performance by an orchestra or quintet. The project connects his interest in music with his curiosity about what new tools make possible when revisiting earlier creative work. More about his research and music is available on his [website](https://brando90.github.io/brandomiranda/).

## Release description

"The Day That Never Comes" began as a hand-written arrangement for a high school orchestra in 2010. Sixteen years later, Brando Miranda gave that arrangement to Suno, an AI music tool, and asked for a bebop jazz reading of it. This single is the result: a 3 minute 2 second small-ensemble jazz take on Metallica's song.

It is a cover of a Metallica composition, made as a personal music project. Distribution rights remain to be confirmed; describing the project as personal does not establish those rights. It is not a recording of an orchestra, and the audio was generated with Suno, not recorded in a studio.
