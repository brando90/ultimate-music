# For fun first, for profit later: what we said and what the sources say

Written Oct 27, 2026 from the WhatsApp conversation with Brando that morning (08:54 to 09:36), then checked against the distributors' and Suno's own pages the same day. Fees change, so re-check the live pages before paying. This is not legal or tax advice.

## 1. The plan Brando asked for

Brando wants to upload to Spotify **for fun first**, and maybe **for profit later**. Both tracks run on the same music and the same artist name.

- **For fun (nonprofit, no cost):** RouteNote's free tier. Upload one track, hear it on Spotify, pay nothing. It tests whether the music is good enough to put out.
- **For profit (later):** a paid distributor, DistroKid, so he keeps 100% of royalties. Keep the same ISRC codes (track IDs) when switching so stream counts carry over.

Spotify takes no direct uploads. Everything goes through a distributor, then the artist claims the profile in Spotify for Artists (free) with their normal Spotify account.

## 4. Metallica: the cover-song license

A Metallica arrangement for orchestra is a new recording of someone else's composition. That makes it a cover for distribution purposes, even though the arrangement is Brando's own work.

**Correction to what Brando was told at 09:16.** RouteNote does not sell a cover license. Its support pages say:

- To **Spotify**, Deezer, Pandora, Tidal, iHeartRadio, Nuuday, Anghami, KKBOX and JioSaavn, a cover can go **without** a mechanical license.
- For other stores, the release has to **exclude the USA, Canada, Mexico, India and Pakistan** unless a license is uploaded.
- To reach every store in every territory, upload a mechanical license (PDF under 10 MB) when creating the release. RouteNote recommends **Affordable Song Licensing**, about **$12 per song plus royalties**, covering US digital and physical sales.
- The cover must carry the **exact original title**. Credit the original songwriters in the C line, and only the performers in the P line.
- Korean services need written permission from the original artist. Karaoke releases are refused. Near-identical soundalikes can be refused by some stores.

DistroKid sells the license itself, about $12 per cover song per year.

**What to do for the first Metallica upload:** the first fun release can go to Spotify through RouteNote with no license, with the excluded territories above. For a release meant to earn money, or to reach the US on every store, buy the mechanical license first. Ask a copyright lawyer before relying on this for money; arrangements and recordings can raise other questions (sync, lyrics).

Sources: [RouteNote: rights to distribute a cover](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/how-do-i-get-the-rights-to-distribute-a-cover/), [RouteNote: mechanical license uploads](https://routenote.com/blog/distributing-covers-got-easier/), [RouteNote: upload cover songs](https://routenote.com/blog/upload-cover-songs/).

## 5. Suno: free plan versus paid plan

Checked Oct 27, 2026 against Suno's help center and secondary guides.

- **Free plan:** "Songs made on the free plan are only intended for personal, non-commercial use." They cannot be monetized. They can be shared with the Suno link.
- **Pro or Premier:** "Songs downloaded while subscribed are granted commercial use rights," including distribution to Spotify and Apple Music.
- **No retroactive rights:** subscribing later "does not give you retroactive commercial use licensing for the songs made with a free plan by default."
- **Lyrics you wrote yourself are yours** on any plan.
- **Ownership is murkier.** Third-party guides disagree on whether paid users own the songs or only hold commercial rights. Suno's own page tells readers to ask their local copyright office.
- **Unverified:** one guide says that since Sep 3, 2026 a song is only commercial if downloaded under a paid plan, and that free users get a limited number of trial downloads. I did not find that on Suno's own page.

**What this means for Brando.**

- The Suno song https://suno.com/s/hoKp4qmYVfWsQsog is fine for sharing, but if it was made on the free plan it **must not be released for money**, and uploading it to Spotify through any distributor is a commercial-style release. Check which plan was active when it was made.
- For a for-profit release, subscribe to Pro or Premier **before** generating, and download while subscribed.
- Keep a record in `tracks/<song>.md`: the Suno plan, the date, the download date.
- A cover of a Metallica song made in Suno adds the cover license question on top of Suno's terms. Both must pass.

Sources: [Suno help center, Rights and Ownership](https://help.suno.com/en/categories/550145), [Suno: rights after subscribing](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2425729), [terms.law guide](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/suno/).

## 6. Royalties and tax basics

- **Streaming royalties** are paid by the stores to the distributor, then to Brando monthly or quarterly, minus the distributor's cut (15% on RouteNote Free, 0% on DistroKid).
- **Per-stream pay** is roughly $0.003 to $0.005 on Spotify. This is a guess from memory, labeled as such; real payout varies by listener country and plan.
- **Small numbers:** 100,000 streams at $0.004 is about $400 before the distributor's cut. This is arithmetic on the guessed rate.
- **Composition royalties** (the songwriting side) go to the songwriter or publisher through a performing-rights organization and the mechanical license. For a cover, they belong to Metallica's publishers, not Brando. For an original, Brando would register with a PRO (ASCAP, BMI) to collect them.
- **US tax basics, not advice:** music income counts as self-employment or business income, reported on a Schedule C. It can carry self-employment tax. Distributors collect a W-9 and may send a 1099 above a threshold. Keep the distributor fees, license fees, Suno subscription and mastering costs as expenses. A tax professional should confirm.
- **"For fun" path:** a RouteNote Free track can still earn small royalties. Earned income can be reportable even when small, so keep the statements.
