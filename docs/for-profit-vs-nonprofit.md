# For fun first, for profit later: distributor, cover-license and Suno rules

Checked against distributor and Suno pages on Oct 27, 2026. Fees change, so re-check the live pages before paying. This is not legal or tax advice. Each claim carries a label: **verified** means read on the official page, **unverified** means from a third-party blog or from memory.

## 1. The plan

Upload to Spotify **for fun first**, and maybe **for profit later**. Both use the same music and the same artist name.

- **For fun (no cost):** RouteNote's free tier. Upload one track, hear it on Spotify, pay nothing. It tests whether the music is good enough to put out.
- **For profit (later):** a paid distributor such as DistroKid, so all royalties are kept. Keep the same ISRC codes (track IDs) when switching so stream counts carry over.

Spotify takes no direct uploads. Everything goes through a distributor, then the artist claims the profile in Spotify for Artists (free) with their normal Spotify account.

## 2. Audio and artwork targets

WAV 44.1 kHz 16-bit, peaks under 0 dB, about -14 LUFS (Spotify's reference level), cover art 3000x3000 px. Standard practice, repeated in `docs/release-checklist.md`.

## 4. Cover songs (the Metallica arrangement)

A new recording of someone else's composition, such as a Metallica arrangement for orchestra, is a cover for distribution purposes, even when the arrangement is original work.

RouteNote's rules (**verified** on its [support page](https://support.routenote.com/kb-article/how-do-i-get-the-rights-to-distribute-a-cover/) and [mechanical-license blog](https://routenote.com/blog/distributing-covers-got-easier/)):

- RouteNote does **not** sell a cover license.
- Covers can go to Spotify, Deezer, Pandora, Tidal, iHeartRadio, Nuuday, Anghami, KKBOX and JioSaavn **without** a mechanical license.
- For other stores, either exclude the **USA, Canada, Mexico, India and Pakistan**, or upload a mechanical license (PDF under 10 MB) when creating the release. With a license, the release goes to all chosen stores in all territories.
- RouteNote recommends **Affordable Song Licensing** (ASL), "$12 per song, plus royalties", covering US digital and physical sales. RouteNote's page is older than ASL's own.

### Affordable Song Licensing, checked Oct 27 2026 (read-only, nothing bought)

- **Site:** https://www.affordablesonglicensing.com. The "Get a License" button leads to https://www.affordablesonglicensing.com/users/sign_up (account first, then your details, the song, payment, then the license certificate). There is no public checkout URL: payment sits behind the account. Pricing page: https://www.affordablesonglicensing.com/pricing. Support: support@affordablesonglicensing.com, (323) 776-6475.
- **Price (verified on ASL's own pricing page):** "$13 + Royalties PER SONG. That's it!" The $13 is ASL's fee. RouteNote's page and an older ASL blog post still say $12; the live page says $13.
- **Royalties (verified):** paid on top of the $13 and "calculated based on the length of your song and the quantity of units you are licensing". The rate is set by US copyright law, the same for every agency. ASL's support page listed $0.124 per copy for songs of 5:00 or less (that page is old; a third-party source gives 13.1 cents for 2026, unverified). The total shows live while entering the quantity. Example: 100 units is about $13 in royalties, so about $26 in all; 15 units or fewer keeps the total near $15.
- **One time or renewing:** ASL's pricing page describes no term, renewal or expiry, and no annual fee ("annual memberships" are not offered). **Unverified:** the license term and any renewal; ASL's support articles and legal page returned nothing readable from here (403). Ask support before paying if it matters. A third-party agency (LANDR) says its own cover license never needs renewing; that is not evidence for ASL.
- **Streaming only needs no license, per ASL:** ASL's blog says "you won't need a license if you are distributing only to streaming platforms" and that downloads (iTunes, Amazon) need one. This matches RouteNote's rule that covers go to Spotify without a mechanical license.
- **Brando's cap:** one time, $15 maximum, nothing yearly or recurring. The $13 fee leaves only about $2 for royalties, which is roughly 15 licensed copies. Since the first release is Spotify only, the cheapest correct route is **no license, $0**, with download stores in the excluded territories left out.
- The cover must carry the exact original title. Credit the original songwriters in the C line and only the performers in the P line.
- Korean services need written permission from the original artist. Karaoke releases are refused. Some stores refuse near-identical soundalikes.

DistroKid sells a cover license itself (about $12 per cover song per year, **unverified**).

First fun release: it can go to Spotify through RouteNote with no license, with the territories above excluded. A release meant to earn money, or to reach the US on every store, needs the mechanical license first. Ask a copyright lawyer before relying on this for money; arrangements and recordings can raise other questions (sync, lyrics).

## 5. Suno: free plan versus paid plan

From Suno's [help center](https://help.suno.com/en/categories/550145) (**verified**):

- **Free plan:** "Songs made on the free plan are only intended for personal, non-commercial use." They cannot be monetized. They can be shared with the Suno link.
- **Pro or Premier:** "Songs downloaded while subscribed are granted commercial use rights," including distribution to Spotify and Apple Music.
- **No retroactive rights:** subscribing later "does not give you retroactive commercial use licensing for the songs made with a free plan by default."
- **Lyrics you wrote yourself are yours** on any plan.
- Copyright ownership of AI output is region-dependent; Suno tells readers to ask their local copyright office.

**Unverified:** third-party guides disagree on whether paid users own the songs or only hold commercial rights ([guide](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/suno/)). One guide claims that since Sep 3, 2026 a song counts as commercial only if downloaded under a paid plan, with a limited number of trial downloads for free users; this was not found on Suno's own pages.

**Consequences:**

- A song made on the free plan must not be released for money, and uploading it through any distributor is a release.
- For a for-profit release, subscribe to Pro or Premier **before** generating, and download while subscribed.
- Record the Suno plan, generation date and download date in `tracks/<song>.md`.
- A Suno-made cover of a Metallica song must pass both the Suno terms and the cover-license rules.

## 6. Royalties and tax basics

- **Streaming royalties** are paid by the stores to the distributor, then to the artist monthly or quarterly, minus the distributor's cut (15% on RouteNote Free per the unverified figure above, none on DistroKid).
- **Per-stream pay** on Spotify is roughly $0.003 to $0.005. **Unverified, from memory;** real payout varies by listener country and plan. For scale, 100,000 streams at $0.004 would be about $400 before the distributor's cut (**unverified**, arithmetic on that guessed rate).
- **Composition royalties** (the songwriting side) go to the songwriter or publisher through a performing-rights organization and the mechanical license. For a cover they belong to the original publishers, not the arranger. For an original, register with a PRO such as ASCAP or BMI to collect them. **Unverified.**
- **US tax basics, not advice (unverified):** music income counts as self-employment or business income, usually reported on a Schedule C, and can carry self-employment tax. Distributors collect a W-9 and may send a 1099 above a threshold. Distributor fees, license fees, a Suno subscription and mastering costs are deductible business expenses. A tax professional should confirm.
- A RouteNote Free track can still earn small royalties. Earned income can be reportable even when small, so keep the statements.
